# PIX-7 and Elsewhere

PIX-7 is a separately developed adversarial audit methodology. Elsewhere's native
AUDIT shares the working principle **structure is what survives perturbation**.
Survival under particular tests is evidence about those tests, not automatic
proof of truth or universal validity.

Elsewhere does not require PIX-7, reproduce its full methodology, or define its
stages. The native audit in [MODES.md](MODES.md) is sufficient to run the spiral.
Use an independently supplied PIX-7 procedure when a participant explicitly
chooses a full PIX-7 audit.

```text
GENERATE → AUDIT → RECOVER → RESEED
```

GENERATE covers opening, branching, and exploratory disturbance. AUDIT can be
native or supplied by the external method. RECOVER extracts continuation value
from its findings; RESEED installs that candidate through SEED.

## Optional handoff

Give the auditor the artifact, its epistemic status, its intended use, the
central relations or claims, known assumptions, and the transformations already
tried. Preserve branch differences; do not submit only the most flattering fold.
Ask the auditor to return the attacks performed, failures, surviving structure,
evidence limits, and unresolved questions in addition to whatever its own
procedure requires.

Then recover from that record. A failed claim may become a narrower hypothesis,
a counterexample, or a question about the missing mechanism. Keep its changed
status visible. Return it to the participant before installing a next seed when
working interactively.

Example: "shared thresholds preserve agency" is challenged by a threshold that
creates social pressure. Recover "an exit usable without penalty" as a design
question. Neither the original slogan nor its replacement is immune to further
attack. This handoff example is not a completed PIX-7 audit.
