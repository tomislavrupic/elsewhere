# Two maps, one place

A worked illustration of transfer with a recoverable mapping.

## SEED and OPEN

```text
SEED("two incompatible maps describe the same place")
OPEN(seed, distance=far)
```

Open incompatibility as competing routes, different coordinate rules, or different
permissions to move. Do not assume one map is a mistake.

## BRANCH

- **Navigation:** one map measures shortest distance, another routes around seasonal
  closures. The same destination has different usable paths.
- **Architecture:** a public floor plan and an accessibility plan disagree about
  which connected rooms form a route. Adjacency does not guarantee passage.
- **Music:** two performers share a score position but use different rules for
  when they can advance. One counts beats; another waits for a partner's silence.

## TRANSFER and TRANSLATE

```text
TRANSFER(navigation, domain=music,
         preserve="a depicted connection may not be a usable transition")
TRANSLATE(music-branch, medium=diagram,
          preserve="advance depends on permission as well as position")
```

Mapping: locations become phrases; paths become transitions; closures become
conditions that prevent entry. The diagram has nodes for phrases and edges
annotated with each performer's entry rule. Geography and music differ: a
performer can wait within a phrase; a blocked physical path may require retreat.
Name that loss rather than claiming equivalent systems.

## DISTURB → FOLD

Remove shared timing. A common score position no longer supplies synchronization.
The performers need an observable handoff, or the branch fails as a coordinated
piece. The fold is **shared labels do not guarantee shared transition rules**.
Preserve descriptive disagreement versus actual inability to coordinate.

## AUDIT

Counterexample: two maps can disagree only in typography while encoding identical
routes. Incompatibility must affect a named relation, not merely appearance.
A coordinated performance is a proposed artifact; no performance was run here.

## RECOVER → SEED′

"A shared place with incompatible rules of entry."

This can generate interfaces, negotiated access, or a new score. It preserves
transition conflict while shedding the requirement that the artifact be a map.
