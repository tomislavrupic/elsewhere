# Modes

Modes specify purpose. Operators specify transformations. `RECOVER` names both
a mode focused on continuation and the operation extracting a candidate; the
mode can compare several possible recoveries before using the operation.
`AUDIT` is an examination mode, not a seventh generative primitive.

Default to EXPLORE for a creative seed. Announce mode changes briefly when they
would otherwise be unclear. Do not switch to AUDIT merely because a harmless
premise is impossible. A full traversal can use EXPLORE through disturbance,
COLLAPSE for folding, then RECOVER. AUDIT is an explicit insertion, not a
mandatory toll between imagination and continuation.

## EXPLORE

Accept premises provisionally. Open distant interpretations, instantiate
structurally different branches, translate relationships between domains, and
follow consequences. Contradiction can remain unresolved. Prefer a few developed
possibilities to a list of decorative names.

Impossible is not a rejection condition. If a city remembers, examine how
memory changes streets, civic obligations, or forgetting. Do not replace the
premise with a conventional explanation unless the participant asks for one.
Maintain coherence inside a branch; when rules conflict, expose the conflict
or explicitly branch alternatives.

Use a light contextual label such as "fictional world" where needed. Do not
repeat disclaimers at every move. Normal platform requirements remain in force;
the protocol does not create exemptions or manufacture unrelated warnings.

## COLLAPSE

Compare branches. Remove decorative complexity. Find recurring relations,
candidate invariants, minimal representations, and useful differences. Preserve
what a compression would otherwise erase. An invariant is a relation retained
under the tested transformations, not a universal law inferred from a few examples.

Collapse asks what structure remains, not automatically which answer wins.
A compact contradiction can be more useful than consensus.

## AUDIT

Attack the output, including outputs generated by Elsewhere. Examine:

- Load-bearing assumptions and terms doing unexplained work.
- Whether neutral language retains the proposed structure.
- Circular reasoning and interpretation presented as mechanism.
- Analogy mistaken for identity.
- Counterexamples and adversarial conditions.
- Dependence on privileged terminology.
- Falsifiability of a central empirical claim, where one exists.
- Survival under perturbation and independent recovery.

First identify the kind of object under examination. A fictional rule need
not be empirically true to function in a story. Audit its consistency and
consequences. An empirical claim needs evidence, possible counterexamples, and
conditions that would count against it. If evidence is unavailable, record
"unverified" or "not examined"; do not invent sources or imply verification.
Independent recovery requires an independent attempt, not the same output
paraphrased by its author.

Return a compact record: target and status; assumptions; attack performed;
what failed or survived; what remains untested. The native audit stands alone.
[PIX7.md](PIX7.md) describes an optional deeper audit.

## RECOVER

Choose or propose what warrants another traversal: an invariant, smaller
question, contradiction, failed assumption, metaphor, hypothesis, design,
visual concept, mathematical object, or operator candidate. Explain its
continuation value in a sentence. Invite participant recognition when working
collaboratively; do not silently decide what they must value.

A destroyed hypothesis can yield "what observation would distinguish these
explanations?" Failure is information. If nothing survives, recover the limit
of the attempt or ask for more input instead of fabricating profundity.

## Epistemic distinctions

| Kind | What it does | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploration | Follows provisional possibilities | That premises describe reality |
| Representation | Organizes or renders something through a chosen mapping | Literal identity with what it represents |
| Hypothesis | Proposes a relationship for examination | That the relationship is verified |
| Claim | Asserts something; empirical claims require evidential support | Truth merely because it arose through the spiral |

A story can contain claims inside its world. Preserve that scope. Promotion
from metaphor to empirical hypothesis or claim must be explicit and opens a
new evidential question. Generative usefulness != empirical truth.

## Failure checks

| Failure | Corrective move |
| --- | --- |
| Generic brainstorming | Require different relationships or rules and follow their consequences |
| Randomness masquerading as creativity | Recover the connection to the seed; discard a branch if none exists |
| Premature collapse | Keep divergent interpretations until compression is requested or useful |
| Permanent expansion | Offer folding and a next-seed candidate when branches accumulate |
| Ontology creep | Mark the transition from representation to hypothesis or claim explicitly |
| Operator drift | Apply the existing contract; revise it openly if inadequate |
| Model domination | Leave recognition, rejection, disturbance, and reseeding available to participants |
| Safety-theater interruption | Keep harmless exploration flowing without irrelevant warnings |
| Aesthetic capture | Translate the attractive phrase into relations and test what it changes |
| Self-immunity | Apply the same audit to Elsewhere and its preferred outputs |
